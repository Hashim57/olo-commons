# Zeus data card

What Zeus trains on, where each part comes from, under which licence, and what it is for. Counts change every run; `zeus/data/meta.json` (gitignored, printed by every build) has the current ones, and `STATUS.md` records what each measured version trained on.

## Task

Claim verification: given one claim and two to four passages with provenance, answer `supported`, `contradicted` or `not_covered`, quote the evidence span and give a one-sentence rationale, as one JSON object (format in `README.md`). Three parts feed it, all through `train_mlx.py` and `train_lora.py`; nothing but the Commons eval split is ever scored.

## 1. Commons examples (rule-made)

Built by `build_dataset.py` from the live Olo Commons through the public `olo_export` RPC. Each atom carries its source licence (Wikipedia CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikidata CC0, arXiv abstracts under the arXiv licence, PubMed abstracts as published with public-domain citation data, Project Gutenberg public domain, Internet Archive as stated per item, Data.gov open licences only). Supported examples take a sentence of an atom as the claim; contradicted examples change one relation word, year, number or name by rule; not_covered examples use passages far from the claim, plus near misses in the train split (passages that share words with the claim but do not state it). The eval split is frozen by a stable hash of the atom id and never receives near misses, gold or imported lines, so versions stay comparable; the harder patterns go into separate frozen splits with their own generators (eval2 and eval3, below), never into it.

Known limits: rule-made contradictions change one token, so the model can learn to spot an odd value rather than read; multi-passage support does not occur in training (eval3 measures it, below); the Commons is English-heavy with a growing Spanish share.

Atoms the build leaves out (since 3 Nov 2026, `zeus/quality.py`, counted in `meta.json` as `atoms_dropped`): an id the export listed twice (the keyless export repeated the last atom of every page until olo-query kept the cursor's microseconds), a stub (a label, one line repeated, or nothing beyond the title and authors: the rule is the ingester's `isStub`, and a third of the archive_org atoms stored before the ingester had it were stubs such as "new book new book"), and a Wikidata item that describes a Wikimedia page rather than a thing in the world (a category, a template, a disambiguation page). They are dropped before any split, so a stub is neither a claim nor a passage in any split. On the 3 Nov 2026 snapshot that was 10 duplicates, 330 stubs and 35 housekeeping items out of 5,000 atoms; 104 of them had been claims or passages before. Since 8 Nov 2026 `atoms_dropped.stub_by_rule` names the rule that refused each stub (`label`, `repeat`, `link_notice`, `catalogue_record`, `shelf_mark`, `rights_notice` since 11 Nov 2026, `file_record` since 15 Nov 2026 (a quoted file name and the names of its collections, the Indian digitisation uploads on the Internet Archive), `no_rest`; the ingester's rules in the ingester's order), so a build can say which rule is doing the work: on the 8 Nov 2026 snapshot, 7,000 atoms in, 835 stubs were 360 catalogue records, 319 labels, 72 shelf marks, 55 link notices, 28 with nothing beyond title, authors and physical description, and 1 repeat. Since 10 Nov 2026 the filter runs before the atom cap (`cap_usable` in `build_dataset.py`; `meta.json` records the rows read as `atoms_read`): until then the cap took the oldest 7,000 rows and the filter then dropped 877 of them, so the build saw 6,123 atoms; the same rows now give 7,000 usable ones, and the eval splits grow with them, so a version measured after the change is compared with one measured before it only indicatively. Since 11 Nov 2026 the cap has a mode (`--cap-mode`, recorded in `meta.json` as `cap_mode` with `max_atoms`): `oldest` (the default, unchanged: the first 7,000 usable atoms in export order, which is what every measured version was built on), `newest` (the last 7,000, so a build follows the Commons and the whole set turns over), or `hash` (the 7,000 with the lowest `cap_rank`, a stable sample over the Commons' whole history that changes only at the edge of the cut as atoms are added; `cap_rank` reads other hex digits of the id's SHA-256 than the eval split does, so the eval share does not move with it). The Commons passed the cap on 10 Nov 2026 (8,229 usable atoms of 9,080 on the 11 Nov snapshot), so under `oldest` no atom added since then enters a build; which mode to train with is an open decision in STATUS.md. On the 11 Nov snapshot the three modes give 34,257 / 33,610 / 34,085 rule-made train examples and 2,141 / 2,148 / 2,072 eval examples; the splits differ, so a version built in one mode is compared with one built in another only indicatively. Since 12 Nov 2026 an atom whose title, summary or content carries an email address is dropped too (`has_email_address`, counted as `atoms_dropped.email`): the Commons stores no personal data, and the 35 such atoms on the 12 Nov snapshot (34 archive_org, 1 openalex) were uploaders' contact lines and a repository's take-down notice, none a fact about the item; the ingester refuses them from the same date (`hasEmailAddress` in text.ts) and sql/017 removes the stored ones. Since 14 Nov 2026 a court record is dropped too (`is_court_record`, counted as `atoms_dropped.court_record`): a text that names a court, a case and the parties is a court order naming private people as accused and petitioners; the 78 on the 14 Nov snapshot were scanned Indian High Court orders mirrored on the Internet Archive under CC BY 4.0 (75 matched by the rule, no atom of any other source), the ingester refuses them from the same date (`isCourtRecord` in text.ts, and the mirror's identifiers in sources.ts) and sql/018 removes the stored ones.

Name edits that are not a name swap (filter `name-check-v1`, since 23 Nov 2026). The rule-made name contradiction swaps any capitalised word of the sentence for the title of a random other atom. Read after v29's per-edit report put name edits lowest (0.889 on 27): most are not a name swap. The title put in is a paper or article title ("Utah Man pleads guilty in wife's death a decision model catch these risks at runtime?"), sometimes cut inside its bracket ("Gaitana (singer"), or the word taken out is a sentence opener such as "However" or "Improving" rather than a name. Such a claim is nonsense, not a contradiction of its passage. `quality.malformed_name_edit` flags the clear cases (a title of five or more words, with a colon, question or exclamation mark or a final full stop, or an unclosed bracket; or one word that opens a sentence of the passage and is a common opener or carries a common-word ending) and leaves a readable swap alone, even across types ("Nietzsche" to "Corbin station"). On the 23 Nov live build it flags 254 of the 336 name edits in the frozen first split and drops 2,076 rules-v1 rows from train (1,853 titles, 659 openers; a row can have both), so contradicted train rows go from 9,892 to 7,816; `meta.json` records `train_filters` and `train_dropped_malformed_name`, and `--keep-malformed-names` reproduces the earlier train split. The eval files are byte-identical: the first split keeps its lines, and `eval.score_by_edit` scores the flagged ones as `name_malformed`, apart from `name`, so from v30 a "not covered" answer to a nonsense claim is no longer read as a missed name contradiction. The rule itself is unchanged, because it also writes the frozen first split.

### 1b. Harder train negatives (generator `hard-v1`, added 26 Oct 2026)

Contradicted and not covered were the labels Zeus found hardest on the held-out VitaminC set, and VitaminC is built from minimal, contrastive edits: one fact changed, or a claim that sits right next to its evidence without being stated by it. `add_hard_negatives()` in `build_dataset.py` adds that kind of example to the **train split only**. For each train atom it picks one English sentence and emits a contrastive set over the very same passages (the atom itself plus two distractors from other train atoms, in a shuffled order): the sentence as `supported`, and up to one `contradicted` and one `not_covered` twin. The model sees the same passages three times and only the edit differs, so it has to read the edit, not the topic. Every hard example keeps the true passage among its passages, which the older not_covered examples never do.

Contradicted twins (one fact changed; the evidence quotes the original sentence, an exact substring of the passage):

- `hard_antonym_flip`: one lower-case word swapped for an antonym the first FLIPS list does not have (rose/fell, allow/forbid, earlier/later, win/lose, activates/inhibits, improves/worsens, public/private, majority/minority, less to more).
- `hard_order_swap`: two values or names that both stay in the sentence trade places ("rose from 10 to 20" to "rose from 20 to 10", "12 and 19 days, respectively" swapped, "A is longer than B" to "B is longer than A").
- `hard_negation`: one negation inserted or removed grammatically ("has been" to "has not been", "will open" to "will not open", "did not approve" to "did approve", "wasn't" to "was").
- `hard_value_shift`: a year moved by one to three (never past 2026), a month moved to a nearby month, or a number moved by a fifth to a half of itself with the same number of digits (percentages stay at most 100), so the value stays plausible.
- `hard_entity_swap`: a year or a name replaced by another of the same kind that the same passage mentions elsewhere ("studied in Paris" to "studied in Krakow" when the passage names Krakow). Names must sit in the same slot (the same preposition before both).
- `hard_quantifier`: "All X" to "No X", "all of" to "none of", "none of" to "all of", "most of" to "few of".

Not covered twins (the claim keeps the sentence's people, places and things but says something no passage states; evidence is empty):

- `hard_modal_shift`: a hedge, plan or correlation stated as certain, done or causal ("may reduce" to "will reduce", "is associated with" to "causes", "was proposed" to "was adopted", "suggests that" to "proves that").
- `hard_scope_shift`: the finding carried to a group or place never mentioned ("in mice" to "in humans", "in adults" to "in children", "in Europe" to "worldwide", "in France" to "in France and Japan").
- `hard_relation_shift`: the same entities in a relation the passage neither states nor rules out ("film directed by" to "film produced by", "studied at" to "taught at", "was nominated for" to "won", "member of the band" to "founder of the band").
- `hard_added_clause`: the true sentence plus a qualifier no passage supports, only on sentences that report an event (Wikipedia and other sources) or a finding (arXiv, PubMed), so it reads as a plausible extra detail ("..., according to a later independent review", "... despite strong public opposition", "..., a result later replicated in a second independent cohort").
- `hard_hedge_drop` (`hard-v2`, 18 Nov 2026): an encyclopedic hedge removed, so the claim states as fact what the passage only reports as reputed, alleged, believed or possible ("Reputedly, she was the model for Diana Scott" to "She was the model for Diana Scott", "is believed to have been built around 1847" to "was built around 1847", "According to legend, the town was founded by" to "The town was founded by", "may have been born in Leeds" to "was born in Leeds"). The research-register hedges ("may reduce", "is associated with") belong to `hard_modal_shift`; this rule covers the Wikipedia and Wikinews register (reputedly, allegedly, reportedly, supposedly, apparently, probably, according to legend or tradition, is said or thought or believed to be or to have been, may or might have been). Refused when the sentence is negated, inside a quotation, when a second hedge of any kind would be left in place, in a sentence about violent crime (an "allegedly" dropped there would state a named person's guilt as fact), and when a passage states the plain sentence anyway (the hedged sentence itself is taken out of the texts before that check, since the plain sentence is a substring of it when the hedge led it). Added because eval3's `modal_shift` pattern stayed the weakest at 0.667 in v24 and the hand-written gold set kept needing hedge examples (gold-2026-11-17 and gold-2026-11-18 each carry one). Since 22 Nov 2026 it also drops the hedges of a conclusion, which abstracts and articles both use ("appears to be clinically applicable" to "is clinically applicable", "seem to be the oldest" to "are the oldest", "is likely to be" to "is", and the past and perfect forms); a word the drop itself removes ("likely") no longer counts as a second hedge. Five rows on the 22 Nov live build (41 to 46 hedge_drop rows), and three other atoms' twins change kind because the hedge rule now claims their sentence; the eval files are byte-identical.

Guards against label noise, each covered by a test in `zeus/tests/test_hard_negatives.py`:

- An edit is never made inside a quotation, never right after a negation or a hedge (not, never, may, might, can, could, or), and never in a sentence that asks or supposes rather than asserts (whether, if, hypothesis). Negation is inserted only into a sentence with no negation and removed only from a sentence with exactly one, so there are no double negatives.
- A contradiction is dropped when the new claim, or the edited span with two words of context on each side, is written in any passage of the example (the passage might state both). An antonym flip is dropped when the antonym is already in the sentence, and a word that is part of a name ("Ulmus minor", "Upper Silesia") is never flipped. A shifted value is dropped when the new value appears in any passage. Identifiers ("Apollo 11"), variables ("t = 2"), days of the month, year ranges, decades and confidence levels are never shifted.
- A not covered twin is dropped when any content word it adds (or that word's stem, so "humans" also catches "human") occurs in any passage of the example, or when the new claim is in a passage. Scope and modal shifts are refused in negated sentences and after "only"; relation shifts need their context word ("film", "album", "band", "patients") so "produced by" never becomes "directed by" for a car.
- Not English sentences (the Spanish Wikipedia atoms) are skipped.

These are still rules. They catch the common failure cases, not all of them: a name swap matched only by its slot can put a person where a place was, an added clause can read oddly on an unusual sentence, and a not covered twin can occasionally be false by world knowledge (which does not change its label, since Zeus judges against the passages). Hand-written gold lines and VitaminC remain the human-grade part of the data.

Determinism and versioning: each atom draws from its own generator seeded by its id, and the hard examples are inserted into the train order by their own generator, after the near misses. Nothing draws from `build()`'s generators, so `eval.jsonl` and `eval2.jsonl` are byte-identical with or without them, and `--no-hard-negatives` reproduces the earlier train split byte for byte. Every hard example carries `"generator": "hard-v2"` (`"hard-v1"` until 18 Nov 2026), a `kind`, the `edit` it made and a `pair` id shared by its contrastive set; `meta.json` records `train_generators` (`["rules-v1", "hard-v2", ...]`) and `train_hard_by_kind`. A rule change bumps the version: `hard-v2` added `hard_hedge_drop`, and because every atom samples the not covered rules in a seeded order, a fifth rule changes which twin most atoms get, so the whole hard set is new from that day (the eval splits are untouched). In the focused cloud run (`zeus/cloud/recipe.py`) the hard rows are sampled down as before, except the `hard_modal_shift` and `hard_hedge_drop` rows, which are kept whole.

Example counts from an offline build of the 26 Oct 2026 snapshot (1,851 atoms; counts of examples, not scores): 1,725 hard examples in 781 contrastive sets, that is 781 supported twins, 694 contradicted (291 value shift, 255 antonym flip, 92 negation, 51 entity swap, 5 quantifier, 0 order swap, a pattern the snapshot's short summaries rarely contain) and 250 not covered (153 added clause, 47 modal shift, 29 scope shift, 21 relation shift). The rule-made train split grows from 5,766 to 7,491 examples: supported 1,496 to 2,277, contradicted 1,314 to 2,008, not covered 2,956 to 3,206, so the hard sets also pull the labels closer to balance (not covered had been the largest by far, because of the near misses).

### 1c. Composed train examples (generator `compose-v1`, added 5 Nov 2026; `compose-v2` since 16 Nov 2026)

Why: v16 scored 0.0 on eval3's two_passage pattern (59 supported claims whose halves come from two passages) and 0.18 on paraphrase_decade. Every rule-made and hard-v1 supported example was one sentence quoted from one passage, so the model had learned that a supported claim has exactly one quote. `zeus/compose.py` adds, over train atoms only with train distractors only:

- `compose_supported` (supported): a sentence from atom A and one from atom B of the same source in one claim, A and B both among the passages; the evidence quotes both.
- `compose_missing_half` (not_covered): the same claim with B left out of the passages.
- `compose_false_half` (contradicted): the same claim with B's half edited by a hard-v1 contradiction rule; the evidence quotes B.
- `century_supported` (supported) and `century_wrong` (contradicted): "in 1863" restated as "in the 19th century", and as the century before or after (never a century that has not begun; years ending in 00 are skipped).

One train atom in five starts a composed set, one in two may give a century pair. The joints are "; ", ", while ", " Also, " and " In addition, " (English joints only between English sentences), never eval3's ", and", and years become centuries, never decades, so a gain on eval3 after this change measures transfer of the skill rather than a copied template. On the 2026-11-05 snapshot it adds 1,988 examples (773 supported, 773 missing half, 216 false half, 113 and 113 century). `eval.jsonl`, `eval2.jsonl` and `eval3.jsonl` are byte-identical with or without it, and `--no-compose` reproduces the train split without it; `meta.json` records `compose-v1` in `train_generators` and the counts in `train_composed_by_kind`.

`compose-v2` (16 Nov 2026) adds a decade pair beside the century pair, because the skill did not transfer: eval3's paraphrase_decade went 0.18 (v16), 0.6 (v18), 0.438 (v20), 0.25 (v22), the one eval3 pattern still far from the ceiling, while the gold set has carried a dozen decade paraphrases since 9 November.

- `decade_supported` (supported): "in 1863" restated as the third of its decade the year falls in, in one of three wordings per part ("in the early 1860s", "early in the 1860s", "during the early 1860s"; "in the mid-1860s", "in the middle of the 1860s", "during the mid-1860s"; and the same for "late"), which the year entails.
- `decade_wrong` (contradicted): the same sentence, same wording, moved to the wrong part of the decade ("in the late 1860s") or to the decade before or after ("in the early 1870s"; never a decade that has not begun).

The bare decade, eval3's form ("in the 1860s"), is still never written by a rule, so eval3 paraphrase_decade from v23 on measures a taught skill under a different wording, not transfer. One train atom in two may give a decade pair. The version bump reseeds the earlier kinds, so a compose-v2 build draws different two-passage and century pairs than a compose-v1 build of the same atoms did; the eval files are unchanged (byte-identical on the 2026-11-16 snapshot). On that snapshot, at the 7,000-atom cap, compose-v2 adds 3,542 examples (1,152 supported, 1,152 missing half, 340 false half, 232 and 232 century, 217 and 217 decade).

### 1d. Long-document train examples (generator `longdoc-v1`, added 17 Nov 2026)

v23, the first version measured on LLM-AggreFact, scored 54.2 percent balanced accuracy (50 is chance), and exactly 50.0 on four of its eleven datasets: one answer for every claim. LLM-AggreFact gives one document of up to 12,000 characters and often a summary-like claim; every passage Zeus had trained on was cut at 900 characters. `zeus/longdoc.py` joins five to nine train atoms of one source into one document of at most 6,000 characters (the anchor atom never first) and makes a contrastive set over it: a sentence of the anchor (`long_supported`), the same sentence edited by a hard-v1 rule (`long_contradicted`), a sentence of an atom of the same source not in the document (`long_missing`), two sentences of two atoms of the document joined as one claim (`long_summary_supported`), and one sentence of the document joined with one from outside it (`long_summary_missing`). One passage only, as LLM-AggreFact gives. Train atoms only; no LLM-AggreFact text is used, so its test split stays a held-out measurement. On the 17 Nov 2026 snapshot: 1,994 examples (462 long_supported, 148 long_contradicted, 462 long_missing, 460 long_summary_supported, 462 long_summary_missing), documents of 421 to 5,416 characters (median 3,564). eval, eval2 and eval3 are byte-identical with and without it; `--no-longdoc` reproduces the train split without it.

`longdoc-v2` (17 Nov 2026, same day): v24, the first version trained on longdoc-v1, rose on LLM-AggreFact (54.2 to 58.1) but answered "not covered" to 263 of its 333 RAGTruth claims. longdoc-v1 gave as many not_covered as supported claims per document and few contradictions (148, one sentence tried). v2 gives each document two supported claims, one contradiction when any of several of its sentences takes a hard-v1 edit, and one not_covered claim (long_missing or long_summary_missing, alternating). On the 17 Nov snapshot: 1,713 examples, 900 supported (53 percent), 360 contradicted (21 percent), 453 not covered (26 percent).

### 1e. Restated supported claims (generator `restate-v1`, added 27 Nov 2026)

`zeus/restate.py`. For a share of the plain rule-made supported rows (a claim copied word for word from its passage), one more supported row whose claim states the same fact written differently, by exactly one rewrite: a date's order ("July 2, 1991" and "2 July 1991"), a percentage ("38 percent" and "38%"), a thousands separator dropped, an ordinal as a figure ("the fifth" to "the 5th", third to tenth only), a small count as a figure ("eight trials" to "8 trials", before a plural noun), or a bracketed aside of at most 60 characters left out (at most 200 of these, and only when no other rewrite applies). The evidence still quotes the passage's own words, which now differ from the claim's, and the source row stays in the set. Why: in the answer eval of 26 November about 17 of the 22 sentences v22 hid were stated by their passage in other words, and 88 of the mistake pool's 338 rows are supported claims answered not covered. A rewrite never changes which fact is stated, so a row is never label noise; one the passage already contains, or that changes nothing, is not used. On the live build of 27 November: 593 rows (aside 348 before the cap was added, then count, date, thousands, percent, ordinal). Train only; eval, eval2 and eval3 are byte-identical with and without it (`--no-restate`), checked on the live build and in `zeus/tests/test_restate.py`.

### 1g. Bounded numbers (generator `bound-v1`, added 27 Nov 2026)

`zeus/bound.py`. A pair of rows over one sentence that states a number exactly: the same sentence with that number put under a bound, once a bound it meets and once one it does not, the wording identical in both ("561 cases" as "more than 500 cases", supported, and "more than 600 cases", contradicted; or "fewer than 600" and "fewer than 500"). Both quote the sentence as evidence and say the comparison in the rationale ("The passage gives 561, which is not more than 600."). Why: on held-out VitaminC v34 called 47 of 100 contradicted claims not covered (v22: 19), and most of those claims bound a number their evidence gives exactly; a rule-made contradiction only ever replaces a number with another, so "the claim's number is not in the passage" could be learned as the only sign of a contradiction, and a bound whose number is nowhere in the passage then reads as not covered. Never rewritten: a number the sentence already hedges or bounds, a range, a year, money, a figure in brackets or followed by a conversion, a number in a name, a sentence with two candidate numbers, a negation, a hedge or a confidence level, and any sentence that is not English. Two sources: train atoms of the Commons (`add_bounded`, at most 600 pairs; 149 pairs on the 27 November snapshot) and VitaminC's own train sentences from real revisions (`import_corpora.py --bound`, 500 pairs, one per case, at most three per sentence frame, written to `data/imported/vitaminc-bound.jsonl`). The VitaminC dev split is never read. Eval, eval2 and eval3 are byte-identical with and without it (`--no-bound`), checked in `zeus/tests/test_bound.py`.

### 1f. Serving format (training time, since 27 Nov 2026)

Not a generator: `zeus/cloud/recipe.py` `serving_mix`, applied by `modal_train.py` to the rows a run trains on. Until v33 every training row labelled its passages by atom id (eight hex characters) and 85 percent had exactly three passages, while olo-answer labels passages p1, p2, ... and shows the ones a sentence cites, or up to eight. Half of every run's rows are now shown in olo-answer's form: passages relabelled p1..pK in a shuffled order, evidence re-keyed to the new labels, and 40 percent of those padded with unrelated passages from the run's own rows up to a random count of at most eight. Claim, label, quotes and rationale are unchanged. Every run is also scored on the first split in that form (`eval_serving` in eval-external.json, passages padded to six from other eval rows only), and promote.py will not promote a version whose serving score is below 0.95 (SERVING_FLOOR).

## 2. Gold examples (hand-written)

`data/gold/*.jsonl`, validated by `validate_gold.py` on every build and in CI. Written against real atoms with their ids and urls kept, aimed at what rules cannot make: contradictions that change a relation rather than a number, near-miss claims that sit next to a passage without being stated by it, support that needs two passages, paraphrases with no shared wording. Passage texts are the atoms' own text (or excerpts of it, marked with " ... " when cut). Licence: each passage's source licence; the claims and rationales are released with the repository under Apache 2.0.

## 3. Imported open corpora

`import_corpora.py` converts open fact-checking corpora into the task format and writes them to `data/imported/` (gitignored; rebuilt before each training run, `IMPORT_N` lines, default 1,500, balanced across the three labels unless `--shares` says otherwise; the cloud run imports 5,000 weighted contradicted 0.40, supported 0.35, not covered 0.25 since mix v3, 27 Nov 2026). Only corpora whose licence allows redistribution and use in a model that serves a product are imported.

| Corpus | Used | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| VitaminC (Schuster, Fisch and Barzilay, NAACL 2021) | yes | CC BY-SA 3.0 (Wikipedia-derived; LICENSE in the release zip) | 370k human-written claim and evidence pairs over Wikipedia revisions, labels SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NOT ENOUGH INFO, many contrastive pairs where one word of evidence flips the label: exactly the contradictions a rule cannot write. Evidence is inline, so provenance is a page and revision id. |
| SciFact (Wadden et al., 2020) | no | CC BY-NC 2.0 | Non-commercial, and Zeus serves helius-olo.io. Revisit if the licence changes. |
| Climate-FEVER (Diggelmann et al., 2020) | no | not stated with the data | The authors ask for a citation only; without a stated licence it stays out. |
| FEVER (Thorne et al., 2018) | no (indirectly) | CC BY-SA 3.0 | Evidence is given as page and sentence ids that need the Wikipedia dump; VitaminC already carries FEVER-derived pairs with the sentences inline. |

Each imported line keeps `corpus`, `unique_id`, `case_id`, the Wikipedia page title and revision id in the passage url, `licence` and an `attribution` string. Two distractor passages from other rows are added so the shape matches the Commons examples. A held-out imported set (`--split dev`, written as `*-eval.jsonl`) can be scored with `eval.py --eval-file`; it never trains and is not the frozen Commons eval.

## Not in the data

No personal data, no query text from the read API, no text the Commons was not allowed to redistribute. Gaps recorded by `olo-answer` are bags of topic words, never questions, and are not training data.


## eval2.jsonl: the harder frozen split (added 25 Oct 2026)

Built over the same eval atoms as `eval.jsonl` and nothing else, with its own generator, so it never overlaps training and leaves the first split byte-identical. Three patterns per atom where the rules allow: a near-miss not covered (passages on the topic that do not state the claim), a relation-flip contradiction with the passages shuffled (both terms of the comparison present, one word changed), and a supported claim whose true passage comes last of four. The first split saturated at v6 (0.995); this one is the Commons-based number to watch, reported per pattern in docs/ZEUS_SCORECARD.md beside the held-out VitaminC dev set.


## eval3.jsonl: the versioned gold-style frozen split (added 26 Oct 2026, eval3-v1)

Built by its own module, `eval3.py` (called from `build_dataset.py`), over the same eval atoms as `eval.jsonl` and `eval2.jsonl` and nothing else: the claim's atom is an eval atom, and so is every passage beside it (distractors and near misses are drawn from eval atoms only), so no training atom's text appears in it, and the two older splits stay byte-identical. Six patterns, each line tagged with its `kind` so it is reported per pattern, made by rule in the style of the hand-made gold patterns (never copied from them: gold trains, this split only measures):

- `near_miss_names` (not covered): the claim's own atom with the claim sentence cut out, plus near-miss atoms; every name in the claim appears in the passages, but none states it. The rest of the atom is refused when it holds more than half of the claim's content words.
- `relation_flip` (contradicted): one strict opposite swapped (higher/lower, larger/smaller, north/south, first/last, inside/outside, always/never and the like), lowercase words only so names such as North Carolina are never touched, both terms of the comparison still in the passage, passages shuffled. Event verbs (born/died, opened/closed) and negations are left out: "died in Warsaw" is not contradicted by "born in Warsaw".
- `before_after` (contradicted): before/after, earlier/later or earliest/latest swapped.
- `modal_shift` (not covered): "may", "might" or "can" in the passage becomes "will" in the claim; the passage says it is possible, not that it happens.
- `two_passage` (supported): the lead sentences of two atoms from the same source joined into one claim, so neither passage alone supports it; made for one eval atom in four (chosen by a hash of the id) so it does not swamp the rest.
- `paraphrase_decade` (supported): "in 1863" restated as "in the 1860s", which the year entails.

Claims are made only from complete sentences of six words or more that are an exact substring of the passage, do not open with a pronoun or linking word ("It", "However", "Here") and are not in the first person, so every evidence quote is exact and every claim stands on its own. Every line passes `validate_gold.check_example`.

Frozen and versioned: the split rule is the same stable hash of the atom id; each atom's choices are seeded by the version, the pattern and the atom id (never the run time or input order), and lines are ordered by a hash, so the same export always gives the same bytes. Each line carries `"version": "eval3-v1"`, and `eval3.manifest.json` beside it records the version, the generator, the split rule, the eval share, counts per pattern and per label, and the sha256 of the file. The content hash moves when the Commons grows (new eval atoms); the version moves only when the rules do: any change to what `eval3.py` writes for the same atoms becomes eval3-v2, enforced by a pinned hash in `tests/test_eval3.py`. Built from the offline snapshot of 26 Oct 2026 (1,851 atoms, summaries of at most 600 characters) it has 58 lines over 46 atoms (near_miss_names 18, relation_flip 6, before_after 4, modal_shift 4, two_passage 22, paraphrase_decade 4); a live build, with longer passages, makes more.

Known limits: rule-made, so the same caveats as the other splits apply in a milder form; a near miss is checked by word overlap, not by a reader, so a rare line may in fact be stated in other words; the joined two-passage claims pair unrelated facts. Not yet scored: no version has been measured on it, and the scorecard shows it as not yet measured until a training run scores it (`eval_v3` in `eval-external.json`, beside eval and eval2, which are scored as before).
