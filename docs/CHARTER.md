# Technology aligned with life: the Helius Network charter

Helius Network builds Olo, Zeus, Olo Commons and POL Net, the parts of WEBOCTA. This charter says what they are for and what they will never do. It is public so anyone can hold us to it. When a product decision conflicts with this charter, the charter wins, and a change to the charter is made in public, with the reason, in this file's history.

Status: draft for Hashim Ruan to adopt. It binds Helius Network only once it is adopted and, where it touches ownership, written into the company's constitution (see docs/OWNERSHIP_OPTIONS.md).

## What we are for

Technology should leave people better able to understand their world, safer from those who would harm them, and better paid for the good work they do. We measure ourselves by that, not by how much of anyone's time we take.

## The commitments

1. **People are not the product.** We sell no data and no attention. We run no surveillance advertising. Olo, Zeus, the Commons and POL Net are paid for by the people and organisations who choose to pay for a service, by grants, and by a disclosed fee on work organisations fund. Never by watching people.

2. **Private by default.** What a person does stays on their device unless they choose otherwise, each time, in words they can understand. No telemetry. Questions asked from POL Net are private. Where we must keep something (a member id, a ledger of money), we keep the least that works and say exactly what it is.

3. **One person, one voice.** POL Net gives each verified person one account, so votes, reviews, reports and earnings cannot be faked by bots or bought in bulk. Each site sees a different id, so being verified never means being followed.

4. **Safety before harm, never silent censorship.** We warn before a scam, a fake site or a dangerous download, and explain why. We do not quietly remove what people may read. Takedowns on POL Net come from verified people, need several of them, and can be reviewed.

5. **Knowledge you can check.** Olo shows its sources. Zeus checks every sentence and says when it could not. We publish how well Zeus does, whatever the result. Models never train on their own output.

6. **Paid for work, not for attention.** POL Net pays members real money for work that helps people and places. No tokens, no points that might one day be worth something, no pay for views, clicks or installs. Organisations see the fee before they pay; members never pay it.

7. **No endless feeds.** We do not build infinite scroll, streaks, engagement scores or notifications designed to pull people back. A good session with our tools is a short one that solved something.

8. **Protect the people most at risk.** Older people, people new to the internet, people in crisis and children are the first targets of scams and manipulation. Features for them (protected mode, family help) come first, not last, and never become a way to watch them.

9. **Open by default.** Code is open source (Apache 2.0). The Commons is openly licensed. Protocols are documented so anyone can run their own server and leave us if we fail them.

10. **People stay in charge of machines.** Nothing an assistant does that leaves the system happens without a person's confirmation. Memory is saved only with approval. Anything that trains a model needs explicit consent.

11. **Honest about limits.** We say what is built and what is not, what we have measured and what we have not. We never call the system something it is not (see docs/WEBOCTA.md, "What the name must not claim").

12. **Built to last beyond its founder.** Helius Network will choose an ownership structure that keeps this charter safe from a future sale (docs/OWNERSHIP_OPTIONS.md), and will invite the people who use POL Net into its governance as it grows.

## How we check ourselves

Once a year, and before any change of ownership or major funding, Helius Network publishes a short report against each commitment: what we did, where we fell short, and what we will change. Members and users can raise a breach by opening an issue on the public repository; every such issue gets a public answer.

## What success looks like

Not monthly active users or time spent. We count: scams stopped before harm, money paid to members for work that helped someone, questions answered with sources people could check, and organisations that could prove their work was done by real people. Those are the numbers we will publish.
