Hand-written examples, one JSON object per line in any `*.jsonl` file here, same shape as `data/train.jsonl`. These are the highest-value examples Zeus trains on: contradictions a rule could not write, claims that look covered but are not, multi-passage support. Keep provenance on every passage. Never include text you do not have the right to publish. Check a file before committing it with `python3 zeus/validate_gold.py` (every evidence quote must be an exact substring of its passage; `not_covered` has empty evidence).

## Files

- `gold-2026-10-24.jsonl`: 10 examples over 12 PubMed atoms (passage `id` is the first 8 characters of the atom id; `atom_id` carries the full id). Four contradictions that change a relation, not a number (more/less cost-effective, intensifies/weakens, limited/close attention, focus/ignore); three near-miss not_covered claims that sit next to a passage without being stated by it (every year versus episodes, a comparison no passage makes, "can protect" versus "has protected"); two supported claims that need two passages; one supported paraphrase with no shared wording. Passage texts are excerpts read through the public olo-query API (title plus the first three sentences of the summary, each cut at 120 characters, cuts marked with " ... "), so they are shorter than the atoms themselves.
- `gold-2026-10-25.jsonl`: 11 examples over 17 atoms from Wikipedia, arXiv and Project Gutenberg (the first set was all PubMed), with passage texts taken verbatim from the offline snapshot of the Commons (the `snapshot` branch, built 2026-10-25 04:37 UTC; the snapshot text is the summary, at most 600 characters). Five contradictions: two relation swaps where both terms of the comparison appear in the passage (Nevşehir rather than Riva; first with one musician, last with another, so the word "last" is a decoy), one before/after flip, one "persisted" turned into "disappeared", and one that needs inference (a building demolished in 1911 does not still stand). Three near-miss not_covered claims whose every name is in the passage (founded Watts Hospital versus founded Duke University; "originally for Mac OS X" does not imply a Windows version; a study of California and Massachusetts says nothing about New York). Three supported claims a word match cannot settle: one needs arithmetic (27 percent to 59 percent is more than double), one needs two passages (a compound claim about two different atoms), one is a paraphrase (Ghent in 1863 as "Belgium in the 1860s"). Each line carries a `kind` field naming the pattern.
- `gold-2026-10-28.jsonl`: 10 examples over 10 atoms from Wikipedia, Wikinews and data.gov (openly licensed only; OpenAlex and PubMed abstracts with unclear or non-commercial licences were left out), passage texts verbatim from the `snapshot` branch built 2026-10-28 05:37 UTC (the Neumann passage starts after its title sentence, which held a date range written with a dash). Five contradictions with no changed number: two relation swaps where every name and date is in the passage (who directed and who attended; the direction of a township rename), two relation flips (opened rather than closed the season, with "drew to a close" as a decoy; one shared transmission against one per service) and one inference (boxes sealed and moved to counting centres are not counted at the polling place). Three near misses (a comparison the profile makes without reporting its result; a right hospital count with an outcome ranking the dataset does not have; drop boxes and the United States in separate sentences). Two supported claims: one compound claim that needs two passages, one paraphrase joining two sentences. Each line carries a `kind` field.
