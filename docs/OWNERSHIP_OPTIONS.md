# Ownership options for Helius Network

A short paper to help Hashim Ruan choose a structure that keeps the charter (docs/CHARTER.md) safe while letting Helius Network raise money and pay people. It is a starting point for a conversation with a solicitor who advises social enterprises, not legal or tax advice. Figures and rules below were correct to the best of our knowledge in November 2026; check each one with the solicitor and the relevant regulator before deciding.

## What the structure has to do

1. Stop a future owner from selling members' trust: no sale of data, no surveillance advertising, no turning POL Net into an attention business.
2. Let Helius Network take grants (many funders prefer or require a not-for-private-profit structure) and, if needed, investment.
3. Let the founder and staff earn a fair living.
4. Give the people who use POL Net a real say as it grows, without making early decisions impossibly slow.
5. Be cheap and simple enough for a one-person company to run today.

## The three main options

### 1. Community Interest Company (CIC)

A limited company (by shares or by guarantee) with two extra features: a community interest statement approved by the Regulator of Community Interest Companies, and an asset lock, which means assets and profits must be used for the community purpose or passed to another asset-locked body. A CIC limited by shares can pay dividends, subject to a cap set by the Regulator (commonly cited as 35% of distributable profits in aggregate; confirm the current figure). It files a yearly community interest report with its accounts.

Good for: grants from funders who accept CICs, a simple and widely understood form, protection against a sale that strips assets for private gain, modest investment.
Limits: the asset lock does not by itself stop a CIC from adopting a business model the founder would hate (for example advertising) if that model still serves the stated community purpose; the purpose and the articles need careful wording. Equity investors are limited by the dividend cap. Converting out of a CIC later is restricted.

### 2. Steward ownership (a golden share held by a foundation or purpose trust)

An ordinary company limited by shares whose articles separate voting control from economic rights. Control stays with people working in or close to the company ("stewards"). Profits serve the purpose, with a fair return to investors that is capped or time-limited. A golden share, held by an independent foundation or purpose trust, can veto any change to these rules or any sale that would break them. Examples often cited include Patagonia (US) and several European firms; the Purpose network supports companies adopting it.

Good for: investment on fair terms, keeping the company independent and unsellable without the purpose guardian's consent, flexibility for a technology company that must move fast.
Limits: more drafting work up front (articles, the golden-share holder's constitution), less familiar to some grant funders, and the guardian body needs real people willing to serve.

### 3. A co-operative or community benefit society

Registered with the Financial Conduct Authority under the Co-operative and Community Benefit Societies Act 2014. Members own and govern it on one member, one vote, which mirrors POL Net's "one person, one account". Community shares can raise money from members and supporters.

Good for: deep member governance, a strong story for POL Net, community share offers.
Limits: slower decisions, harder to raise conventional investment, more governance work than a one-person company can carry today. It may suit a later stage better than the start.

## A possible path

1. **Now:** adopt the charter publicly. Keep Helius Network as it is, or convert to a CIC limited by shares if grant funders you are applying to prefer it, with a community purpose written around safe, private, verifiable technology for the public benefit, and articles that name the charter's red lines (no sale of personal data, no surveillance advertising).
2. **Before outside investment:** add steward-ownership features (or move to steward ownership outright) with a golden share held by a small independent purpose trust, so investors know the purpose cannot be bought out.
3. **When POL Net has real members:** give members a formal voice. Options include a members' council that elects a seat on the board, member approval for changes to the registry rules and the fee, or spinning the pol:// registry out into a member-owned co-operative that Helius Network serves.

## Questions to take to the solicitor

- Which structure suits the grants currently being applied for (Innovate UK, Startup Wise Guys, others in the project folder), and does any require a specific form?
- How to word the purpose and the articles so the red lines are binding, not just aspirations.
- The cost and time of each option, now and at the next funding round.
- Tax treatment of each option for a small technology company, and for members paid through POL Net.
- How to keep the founder's role and pay fair and transparent under each option.
